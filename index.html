01 / THE CHALLENGE

Solar changes.
Demand changes.
Phase balance must adapt.

Unequal single-phase PV and consumer demand create different net power on the R, Y and B phases.

Manual surveys and physical reconnection cannot continuously follow daytime generation and evening demand. A phase arrangement that works in the morning may become unsuitable later.

Voltage riseVoltage unbalanceNeutral currentReverse power flow
The engineering question

How can feeder-specific modelling and controlled load transfer support rooftop PV integration while respecting electrical operating limits?

02 / ENGINEERING APPROACH

One study. Two connected models.

MATLAB / SIMULINK / STATEFLOW
MODEL A

Controlled phase transfer

An unbalanced network with PV injection and a selected 2 kW single-phase load. A Stateflow controller sequences the static transfer switch.

  • 11 kV / 415 V distribution transformer
  • R, Y and B load and PV variation
  • Current-zero waiting and gate interlocks
MODEL B

Sahajanand feeder assessment

A three-phase, four-wire network with 12 LT sections, informed by measured operating data and a fixed load snapshot.

  • 630 kVA, 22 kV / 415 V DTR in the updated case
  • Unequal PV injection at LT1, LT6 and LT12
  • No-PV baseline V0 versus PV case P25

Control timing and simulation parameters +

The Stateflow chart enables transfer after 50 ms and includes a 10 ms DEAD_TIME state. The waveform shows an approximately 20 ms all-off interval including sequencing and current-zero waiting. The standalone SCR circuit uses a 5 μs discrete step. The STS feeder uses a 1.5 km π-section with R = 0.21 Ω, L = 1.5 mH and C = 16.5 nF. Numerical hysteresis and dwell-time tuning are not fully supplied.
